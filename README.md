# OCD
What exactly is OCD?
OCD is a condition involving two main things:
- Obsessions: Unwanted, repetitive thoughts, images or urges that cause anxiety, fear or discomfort.
- Compulsions: Repetitive actions or mental rituals someone feels driven to perform to reduce the anxiety caused by those thoughts.
It's often a cycle:
OCD doesn't look the same in everyone.

A. Contamination OCD
- Fear of germs, dirt or infections.
- Excessive handwashing.
- Avoiding touching door handles.
- Feeling contaminated after touching certain objects.
- Checking OCD
- Checking whether doors are locked repeatedly.
- Checking whether appliances are switched off.
- Repeatedly checking messages before sending them.
- Fear that you might have caused an accident.
C. Symmetry and ordering OCD
- Feeling uncomfortable when things aren't perfectly aligned.
- Arranging objects in a particular order.
- Feeling that things must be even or symmetrical.
- Intrusive thoughts OCD
This is particularly misunderstood.

Someone may experience disturbing thoughts involving:
- Accidentally hurting someone.
- Sexual thoughts they don't want.
- Religious or blasphemous thoughts.
- Doing something morally unacceptable.
- Fear of being a dangerous or terrible person.
Having these thoughts does NOT mean someone wants to act on them.

E. Relationship OCD (ROCD)

This can involve:
- Constantly questioning whether you really love your partner.
- Repeatedly analysing their behaviour.
- Wondering whether they're the right person.
- Seeking reassurance that they love you.
- Obsessively analysing your own feelings.
3. What does OCD feel like mentally?
Imagine knowing that you've locked your bedroom door.

You remember physically locking it. You even watched yourself do it.

But your brain keeps saying:

"What if you didn't?"

You go back and check.

You feel relieved.

Five minutes later:

"But what if you only thought you checked?"

You check again.

The difficult part is that reassurance doesn't always solve the problem. The brain keeps demanding certainty.
4. Is OCD treatable?
Yes. OCD is treatable.

Common treatments include:
- CBT: Cognitive Behavioural Therapy.
- ERP: Exposure and Response Prevention, a specialised therapy that helps people face anxiety without performing compulsions.
- Medication: Certain antidepressants, particularly SSRIs, can help when prescribed by a qualified clinician.
OCD is not a personality flaw, a lack of discipline or simply overthinking.
